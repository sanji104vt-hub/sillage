Tax-Free Perfume Shopping in Japan: 2026 Change Guide

How tax-free perfume shopping in Japan works through October 31, 2026 and what changes under the refund method from November 1, 2026.

Plan by purchase date: For purchases from November 1, 2026, Japan changes to the refund method: pay the tax-inclusive price, complete customs confirmation at departure within 90 days of purchase, then receive the refund from the store or its refund operator. Purchases through October 31 follow the earlier system.

Two systems in 2026

Check which date applies to your purchase.

Until October 31, 2026

Tax exemption at the store

Eligible non-resident visitors generally present the required passport at a participating tax-free store and complete the current procedure when purchasing. Customs may check tax-free goods when you leave Japan.

For consumables, which include fragrance, National Tax Agency guidance refers to a pledge made at the time of purchase to export the goods within 30 days without opening the designated packaging. Some other official pages omit this figure, so confirm the current requirement with the store.

From November 1, 2026

Pay first, then receive a refund

Under the refund method from November 1, 2026, travelers pay the tax-inclusive amount at purchase. After customs confirms the goods are being taken out of Japan, the tax-equivalent amount is refunded through the participating business's process.

Consumption tax

Which rate applies to fragrance.

Japan's consumption tax has two rates: a standard rate of 10% and a reduced rate of 8%. The reduced rate applies only to food and beverages (excluding alcohol and dining out) and to newspapers published at least twice a week under a subscription contract. Fragrance is not in either category, so the standard 10% rate applies.

The refund amount may be less than the full tax amount. Some retailers or refund operators charge a handling fee. Confirm the refund terms at the point of purchase.

The reduced-rate categories are listed by the National Tax Agency. The source is linked at the end of this guide, so you can check whether an item you plan to buy falls inside them.

Two different systems

Tax-free is not duty-free.

Travelers often use the two words interchangeably. In Japan they refer to different things, and only one of them is what this guide covers.

Tax-free

Stores in the city. The subject is Japan's consumption tax (standard rate 10%). You buy the product, then complete the procedure for taking it out of Japan. This is what Sillage covers.

Duty-free

Shops inside the airport after security. The subject is customs duty and taxes on specific goods such as alcohol and tobacco. The rules, the goods and the procedure are different.

A fragrance counter in a department store is tax-free shopping. A fragrance shop past passport control is duty-free. The price difference between them is not a simple comparison, because the taxes involved are not the same.

From November 1, 2026

The confirmed refund flow from November 1

  1. Buy at a participating tax-free store and pay the tax-inclusive price.
  2. Keep the purchased perfume and the passport or other required travel document available for departure procedures.
  3. Complete customs confirmation at departure within 90 days of purchase, before checking baggage and boarding procedures. Export the goods without delay afterwards.
  4. Present the tax-free goods for customs confirmation when leaving Japan, following the current airport or port instructions.
  5. After confirmation, receive the tax-equivalent refund through the participating business's stated method.
Keep every item on each receipt available: customs confirmation is per purchase transaction (receipt). If even one tax-free item is missing, none of the items in that transaction can receive customs confirmation or a refund. Follow the official airport or port instructions.

At the airport

Go to customs before you check your bag.

Keep all tax-free goods available for customs confirmation and any required inspection. If the perfume is already inside a suitcase that has been handed to the airline, it cannot be presented.

  1. Arrive at the airport with extra time. Customs confirmation cannot be rushed.
  2. Complete customs confirmation before you check your baggage with the airline and before security. Keep all tax-free goods with you, including perfume packed in a suitcase; checked bags cannot be retrieved for this procedure.
  3. Present your passport at a tax-free procedure kiosk or electronic terminal. At Narita, Haneda, Kansai, Chubu, Fukuoka, New Chitose and Naha airports, Visit Japan Web is an alternative in designated international-departure lobby areas connected to the procedure-specific wireless LAN before security.
  4. Follow the result: green means no inspection is required and customs confirmation is complete; red means you must take the goods to the customs inspection area and complete inspection.
  5. Complete customs confirmation within 90 days of purchase and before boarding procedures. For a domestic-to-international connection, use your final departure airport from Japan. Export the confirmed goods without delay.
  6. After customs confirmation, receive the refund from the store or its refund operator under the terms given at purchase. Refund methods, timing and any fees vary; an immediate cash refund at the airport is not guaranteed.
Timing matters. If you arrive close to boarding, the confirmation may not be completed, and a refund may not be possible. Treat the customs step as part of your departure schedule, not as something to do if there is time left.

Fragrance specifics

What changes for perfume.

Fragrance is treated as a consumable under the tax-free system, so the changes to consumables apply directly to it.

The ¥500,000 cap is removed

Through 31 Oct 2026, consumables have a purchase cap of ¥500,000. Under the refund method this cap is removed from November 1, 2026, which matters if you plan to buy several bottles of niche fragrance in one visit.

Special packaging ends

From November 1, 2026, the sealed-packaging requirement for consumables is removed. This is a change to packaging, not permission to use the product.

Using it in Japan

Customs confirmation requires that the goods are leaving Japan. If you open and use the perfume during your stay, you may be unable to complete the confirmation, and therefore unable to receive the refund.

The category itself disappears

Under the current procedure, goods are divided into general goods and consumables, and fragrance sits in the consumables category. From 1 Nov 2026 the distinction between the two categories is removed, so the separate rules that currently apply to consumables no longer apply as a separate set.

The end of special packaging is widely misread. No seal does not mean free to use. The condition that the goods leave Japan is unchanged.

The 90-day rule

Complete customs confirmation within 90 days of purchase.

The deadline applies to customs confirmation at departure, not to receiving your refund. After confirmation, export the goods without delay.

A long stay can put an early purchase outside the confirmation window. Visitor-status eligibility is a separate requirement; a short trip alone does not establish eligibility.

If your stay is long, consider the timing of the purchase in relation to your departure date, and confirm the current conditions with the retailer.

Perfume-specific caution

Tax-free eligibility and aviation rules are separate.

A perfume purchase being tax-free does not mean it can be carried on an aircraft without restriction. Fragrance contains liquid and often alcohol. Check your airline and departure airport rules for cabin baggage, checked baggage, container size and total quantity.

Shipping is not a guaranteed workaround. Since 1 Apr 2025, goods personally mailed overseas by the purchaser do not qualify for the current tax-free procedure. This change is already in force — it took effect ahead of the November 2026 reform, so it applies to purchases made today. A store's own export or delivery service is separate and must be confirmed with that retailer. Sillage does not promise that perfume can be shipped internationally.

Common questions

Questions travelers ask.

When does the Japan tax-free system change?

The refund method starts for purchases made from 1 November 2026. Purchases made through 31 October 2026 follow the current procedure.

Do I pay consumption tax at the store?

Under the current procedure through 31 October 2026, eligible purchases are exempted at the store. From 1 November 2026, travelers pay the tax-inclusive price first and receive a refund later.

Where do I get the refund?

After customs confirmation, the store or its refund operator provides the refund. Methods, timing and any handling fees vary. Confirm the terms at purchase; an immediate cash refund at the airport is not guaranteed.

Is perfume a consumable item?

Fragrance is treated as a consumable through 31 October 2026. From 1 November 2026, the distinction between general goods and consumables and the ¥500,000 consumables cap are removed.

What happens if I use the product in Japan?

Customs confirmation requires that the goods are being taken out of Japan. Removing the special packaging requirement does not mean the product may be consumed in Japan before departure.

What does the 90-day deadline apply to?

For purchases from 1 November 2026, complete customs confirmation at departure within 90 days of purchase, then export the goods without delay. This is not a deadline for receiving the refund. Eligibility also depends on your visitor status; confirm it with the store.

Can I use Visit Japan Web at every airport?

No. It is available for this procedure at Narita, Haneda, Kansai, Chubu, Fukuoka, New Chitose and Naha, in designated international-departure lobby areas connected to the procedure-specific wireless LAN before security. Follow the official instructions; tax-free procedure terminals are also available.

What if one item on a receipt is missing?

Customs confirmation is performed per purchase transaction (receipt). If you do not have even one tax-free item included in that transaction, none of its items can receive customs confirmation or a refund. Keep every item together and available for inspection.

Is tax-free the same as duty-free?

No. Tax-free stores in the city refund Japan's consumption tax. Duty-free shops at the airport relate to customs duty and to taxes on items such as alcohol and tobacco. Sillage covers the former.
